A.5 Distance matrix

A table is taken as a distance matrix, rather than as a table of objects, when it meets all of these conditions: it has at least 3 rows; the header has one more cell than there are rows; the first column repeats, in the same order, the names in the header from the second cell onwards (regardless of case and of leading or trailing spaces); all other cells are non-negative numbers; and the diagonal is zero. If dij and dji differ by more than one part in a million (relative difference), they are replaced by their mean, and the reading message says so. If any condition fails, the table is read as an ordinary table of objects.

1Introduction to ClusteringPro

✓Decision rule · how many objects and how many variables?Section 1.8
SituationReadingWhat to do
At least 5 to 10 objects per expected clusterAdequateThe criteria for k, the silhouette and resampling are informative. With 30 objects and 3 expected clusters you can already work well.
Fewer than 5 objects per clusterLimitedSmall clusters dissolve under resampling and any outlier forms its own cluster. Interpret the tree, not the partition.
More variables than objectsCautionAll the distances become similar to one another. Remove redundant variables or cluster on the first principal components (Block 3).
Variables irrelevant to the questionRiskEvery “noise” variable dilutes the structure carried by the useful ones. Keep them as supplementary: they are not used for clustering, but they are used to describe the clusters.

The app flags the object-to-variable ratio in Block 2 and clusters with fewer than three objects in Block 5.

✓Decision rule · reading the average silhouette widthSection 1.9
Average silhouetteStructureHow to word it
0.71 to 1.00Strong“A strong, well-separated cluster structure was found.”
0.51 to 0.70Reasonable“The partition is clear, with some objects close to the boundaries.”
0.26 to 0.50Weak“The clusters overlap; the partition is exploratory.” Check it against the stability results in Block 6.
≤ 0.25No substantial structureDo not claim that there are clusters. Try another coefficient, another method or another number of clusters, or accept that the data form a gradient.

The thresholds are those of Kaufman and Rousseeuw (1990). In real biological data with many variables, a silhouette of 0.4 often already goes with interpretable clusters.

B1Home and theory

✓Rule · a high value does not mean correct clustersSection 2.2

Single linkage is the only rule that recovers the moons, and yet it has the lowest cophenetic correlation (0.651 against 0.751 for UPGMA). The cophenetic correlation measures how well the tree reproduces the pairwise distances, not whether the clusters are the true ones. Likewise, the k-means silhouette (0.453) is far from negligible even though the partition is absurd: the silhouette rewards compact, round clusters. With curved or elongated shapes, always look at the figure before the index.

✓Decision rule · how the app reads the Hopkins statisticSection 3.4
If…LevelReading
P ≤ 0.05 and H ≥ 0.70✅ clearClear clustering tendency: the data are far from spatial randomness; it is worth looking for clusters.
P ≤ 0.05 but H < 0.70⚠️ modestDetectable but modest: more than chance produces, but not by much. Expect a few well-populated clusters or gradients. With few objects per cluster H remains modest even when the map shows clear clouds: let the map and the VAT have their say.

✓Decision rule · how the grade is computedSection 3.5
LetterConditionThe app’s sentence
ANo red or amber warningsThe data are ready for clustering: clear tendency, balanced variables, no red flags.
BOne or two amber warnings, no red onesGood to go, with the caveats listed below. Keep them in mind when you interpret the clusters.
COne red warning, or more than two amber onesClustering is possible but fragile. Address the flagged points, or treat the results as exploratory.

Counted as red: weak or no tendency, very different spreads without scaling, and fewer than 8 objects. Counted as amber: modest or moderate tendency, extreme outliers or more than 10% suspect objects, redundant variables, different spreads after transformation, more than 20% of rows dropped, and fewer than 3 objects per variable.

B3Similarity and distance

✓Decision rule · what to do according to the diagnosisSection 4.3
If the matrix…then
is Euclidean (negative mass < 1%)Anything goes: Ward, k-means on the PCoA axes, PCoA percentages.
is nearly Euclidean (1–10%)Ward and PCoA are acceptable; the percentages are approximate.
is not Euclidean (≥ 10%)Prefer UPGMA, complete linkage or PAM. If you want Ward or k-means, tick Take the square root of the dissimilarity (√d) on card 1 and recompute (with a loaded matrix, choose Square root on the Supplied matrix tab).
is not metric (violates the triangle inequality)Hierarchical methods work (centroid and median linkage may show inversions); k-means needs coordinates: use PAM or a PCoA.
✓Decision rule · how to read the stress of the mapSection 4.6
StressReading
< 0.10The map reproduces the distances well: you can reason from it.
0.10–0.20Acceptable representation; read distant pairs with caution and confirm on the heat map.
≥ 0.20Poor two-dimensional representation: trust the matrix (heat map, neighbours table, tree), not the map.

Stress is computed after linearly rescaling the map distances, because classical PCoA always shrinks them slightly; this way it measures only the distortion, not that harmless shrinkage.

B4Hierarchical clustering

✓Decision rule · which rule the app proposesSection 5.1
If the Block 3 matrix…then
is Euclidean or nearly so (negative mass < 5%)Ward.D2: compact clusters of similar variance. The alternative is UPGMA if what matters is how faithfully the tree reproduces the distances. With community tables it adds that UPGMA on Bray–Curtis remains the classic choice.
is not Euclidean (negative mass ≥ 5%)UPGMA (average), which needs only the matrix. If you want Ward, go back to Block 3 and tick the square root (√d).
✓Decision rule · how the app reads the cross-tabulationSection 5.4
ARIApp sentence
> 0.80The tree recovers your known grouping almost exactly.
0.50–0.80Substantial agreement with the known grouping, with some objects placed differently.
0.20–0.50Partial agreement: the clusters and the known grouping capture different structure.
< 0.20The clusters are unrelated to the known grouping (an ARI near 0 is what random labels give).

Purity is the proportion of objects that fall into the majority known group of their cluster. A low ARI does not mean that the tree is wrong: it may be finding a different structure, just as real, that the prior classification does not capture.

B5Partitioning and advanced methods

✓Decision rule · which method the app proposesChapter 6
If…it proposes
the input is a distance matrixPAM: it works on the matrix and its medoids are real objects.
the data are mixed, nominal, binary or ordinalPAM, for the same reason: the Block 3 coefficient is not a Euclidean distance between coordinates.

estabilidad		The mean Jaccard coefficient with which each cluster reappears in the bootstrap replicates: above 0.75 it is stable; below 0.5 it dissolves.7.3
Clustering tendency
tendencia al agrupamiento		Whether the data contain clusters or are spread almost uniformly; it is assessed with Hopkins and VAT before clustering.3.4
Connectivity
conectividad		How many of the nearest neighbours of each object ended up in another cluster, with more weight for the nearest ones. Lower is better.7.6
Consensus k
consenso		The number of clusters with the most votes among the thirteen criteria of Block 6.7.1
Cophenetic correlation
correlación cofenética		The correlation between the original dissimilarities and the height at which each pair of objects is joined in the tree: how faithful the tree is to the matrix.5.3
Cramér’s V
V de Cramér		The strength of the association between two categorical variables, from 0 to 1, based on the χ² of their cross-tabulation.8.2
D
Davies–Bouldin index
Davies–Bouldin		The average, over the clusters, of the worst ratio between joint dispersion and distance between centroids. It votes for its minimum.7.1
DBSCAN
DBSCAN		Density-based clustering: it forms clusters from dense regions. An object is a core point if it has at least minPts objects within distance ε; objects that cannot be reached from any core point are noise.6.5
Dendrogram
dendrograma		The drawing of the hierarchical tree: the height of each junction is the dissimilarity at which the clusters were merged.5.5
Dissimilarity and distance
disimilitud y distancia		A number that grows the more two objects differ. A distance is a dissimilarity that also satisfies the triangle inequality.4.1
Dunn index
Dunn		The smallest distance between clusters divided by the largest cluster diameter. It votes for its maximum.7.1
E
Elbow
codo		The point on the curve of the within-cluster sum of squares beyond which adding clusters hardly reduces it.7.1
Euclidean matrix
matriz euclidiana		A dissimilarity matrix that can be represented exactly by points in a Euclidean space (Euclidean-embeddable). Ward and k-means assume one.4.3
External validation
validación externa		Comparing a partition with a known grouping, or two partitions with each other, using indices such as ARI and NMI.7.5
F
Fuzzy c-means
c-medias difuso		A variant of k-means in which each object has a degree of membership in each cluster; the exponent m controls how fuzzy the partition is.6.3
G
Gap statistic
estadístico gap		The difference between the logarithm of the within-cluster sum of squares expected under uniform data and the observed one. It can conclude that there are no clusters.7.2
Gaussian mixture
mezcla gaussiana		A model in which the data come from several normal distributions; it gives each object its probability of belonging to each component.6.4
Gower dissimilarity
Gower		Dissimilarity for mixed data: it averages a difference between 0 and 1 defined for each type of variable.4.2
H
Hellinger transformation
transformación de Hellinger		The square root of the relative abundances at each site. It makes the Euclidean distance suitable for species tables.3.3
Hierarchical k-means
k-medias jerárquico		k-means started from the centroids of the Block 4 tree cut, instead of from random starts.6.1
Hopkins statistic
estadístico de Hopkins		Compares the distances from random points and from real objects to their nearest neighbour. Close to 0.5, the data are no more clustered than random data.3.4
I
IndVal
valor indicador		Indicator value: the specificity times the fidelity of a species in a cluster, with its permutation test.8.3
J
Jaccard coefficient
Jaccard		Similarity between two sets: shared elements divided by the elements present in either. It is used with presence–absence data and to compare clusters.4.2
K
k-means
k-medias		A partitioning method that alternates between assigning each object to the nearest centroid and recalculating the centroids, minimising the within-cluster sum of squares.6.1
Known grouping
agrupación conocida		A prior classification of the objects (region, species, habitat). It is never used to form the clusters: it colours the figures and serves for external validation.3.2
Kruskal–Wallis test
Kruskal–Wallis		A rank-based test of whether a variable differs between clusters; the alternative to ANOVA that does not assume normality.8.1
L
Lance–Williams recurrence
fórmula de Lance y Williams		A general formula that updates the dissimilarities after each merge and reproduces almost all linkage rules; with a free β it gives flexible linkage.5.2
Linear discriminant analysis
análisis discriminante lineal		Finds the combinations of variables that best separate clusters already formed and provides a rule for classifying objects, with posterior probabilities.8.4
Linkage rule
regla de enlace		How the dissimilarity between two clusters is measured when building the tree: nearest neighbour, farthest neighbour, average, Ward, centroid and others.5.2
M
Medoid
medoide		The real object in a cluster with the smallest total dissimilarity to the others; the centre used by PAM and CLARA.6.2
Multivariate outlier
atípico multivariado		An object very far from the centre of the data when all variables are considered at once; it is measured with the squared Mahalanobis distance.3.5
N
NMI
información mutua normalizada		Normalised mutual information: how much one grouping tells about the other, from 0 (nothing) to 1 (the same).7.5
Noise
ruido		In DBSCAN, the objects that are not close to any core point and belong to no cluster; in the files they are coded as cluster 0.6.5
O
Object
objeto		Each row of the table: the unit being clustered (accession, site, sample, profile).1.3
P
PAM and CLARA
PAM y CLARA		Partitioning around medoids, with any dissimilarity; CLARA applies PAM to samples for large tables.6.2
Partitioning
particionamiento		Methods that divide the objects directly into k clusters, without building a tree.6.1
Posterior probability
probabilidad posterior		The probability that an object belongs to each cluster according to a model (Gaussian mixture or discriminant analysis).6.4, 8.4
Principal coordinates
coordenadas principales		Axes that reproduce on a map the dissimilarities of a matrix (classical MDS). With a non-Euclidean matrix, some eigenvalues are negative.4.3
Purity
pureza		The proportion of objects that belong to the most frequent class of their cluster; it favours partitions with many clusters.7.5
R
Rand index and adjusted Rand index
índice de Rand y Rand ajustado		The proportion of pairs of objects that two groupings treat in the same way; the adjusted index subtracts what is expected by chance, and is 0 for random labels and 1 for identical groupings.7.5
Random seed
semilla aleatoria		A number that fixes the randomness of a calculation: with the same seed, the same data and the same settings, the result is repeated.1.6
Resubstitution and leave-one-out
resustitución y dejando uno fuera		The accuracy of a classification rule on the same objects used to fit it (optimistic), or classifying each object with a rule fitted without it (honest).8.4
S
Scaling
escalado		Bringing the variables to comparable units (z-scores, 0–1 range, median and interquartile range) so that none dominates because of its units.3.3
Shepard diagram
diagrama de Shepard		A plot of the original dissimilarities against the distances on the map: the closer the points lie to the diagonal, the more faithful the map.4.6
Silhouette
silueta		For each object, (b − a)/max(a, b): closeness to its own cluster compared with the neighbouring one. The average summarises the partition: 0.7 strong, 0.5 reasonable, 0.25 weak.1.9, 6.7
Specificity and fidelity
especificidad y fidelidad		The two components of the indicator value: what share of a species’ abundance is in a cluster, and in what proportion of the cluster’s sites it occurs.8.3
Stress
estrés		How poorly a two-axis map reproduces the original dissimilarities; it is read together with the Shepard diagram.4.6
Supplementary variable
variable suplementaria		A variable that plays no part in forming the clusters and is used only to describe them; its tests in Block 7 do have inferential value.3.2, 8.1
T
Tanglegram
tanglegrama		Two dendrograms facing each other, with lines joining each object to itself; the crossings show where they differ.5.7
Test value
valor de prueba		The difference between the mean of a cluster and the overall mean, in units of its standard error; |v| ≥ 1.96 indicates a variable that characterises the cluster.8.1
Tree cut
corte del árbol		A horizontal line at a given height, or at a given number of clusters, that turns the tree into a partition.5.4
U
UPGMA
UPGMA		Average linkage: the dissimilarity between two clusters is the mean of the dissimilarities between their objects.5.2
V
Variation of information
variación de la información		A distance between two groupings based on the information lost and gained in passing from one to the other; 0 if they are identical.7.5
VAT
VAT		Visual assessment of tendency: the reordered dissimilarity matrix shown as an image, in which dark blocks along the diagonal indicate clusters.3.4
W
Ward’s method
Ward		Ward’s minimum variance method: the linkage that merges the clusters whose union least increases the within-cluster sum of squares. Ward.D2 applies the criterion to squared distances.5.2
Wilks’ lambda
lambda de Wilks		The proportion of the variation not explained by the discriminant axes: close to 0, the clusters are well separated.8.4
Working matrix
matriz de trabajo		The data after missing values have been handled and the variables transformed and scaled in Block 2; it is the basis of the quantitative coefficients and of the coordinate-based methods.3.3

FThird-party resources

ClusteringPro is an original work: its code, its figure engine, its colour palettes and scales, its illustrations and its practice data were all created for this program. It includes no third-party software components: no libraries, typefaces, images or data files from other sources, and the app makes no requests to the internet. The app and the manual do use some published colour designs, which are declared here together with their terms of use.

iHow the app reads a spreadsheet workbook

An .xlsx, .xlsm or .ods file is actually a compressed archive that stores its content as XML. Block 2 opens it with the app’s own code (js/sheets.js): it decompresses each part with the decompressor built into the browser and reads the XML with the browser’s own parser. That is why the app needs no external library. The old binary formats .xls and .xlsb are not supported: save the workbook again as .xlsx.
